Why this exists. No public source has the full picture. Every aggregator is missing at least two mechanics the in-game tooltips describe plainly, several carry import errors — including costs off by a factor of eight — and several list attachments that do not exist in the game. See the Error Log.

How to build a loadout

Two ways to work through this. Both end at the same verification step.

With an LLM

This document is a model, not a dataset. A model handles the constraint satisfaction well — a dozen interacting tags against a hard 100-point budget is the kind of bookkeeping it is good at — and it recalls base weapon stats closely enough to reason with.

Costs are where it breaks, and specifically in two places: magazines and the headshot and subsonic ammo. Those genuinely vary per weapon, so a model has nothing stable to recall and will produce a plausible number rather than say so. Everywhere else costs are fixed and it is usually right.

Rule 1 is the check. It prices an attachment from its effects, so you can price it yourself and compare. When the two disagree, either it misread an effect or the tooltip contains something neither of you accounted for. For the two variable cases, just read the number in game — there is nothing to derive.

Work with it rather than from it. Ask for two or three builds with different priorities instead of one best answer, have it show the running total, and push back when a number looks wrong. Tell it how you actually play — much of this document is drawbacks that are nearly free to one player and expensive to another.

Instructions for the assistant are at the end of this document.

On your own

Manual is better when you already know the weapon and are iterating on a build you have played, when you are adjusting one slot rather than starting over, or when you are testing a specific pick against feel. It is also faster than describing a weapon you have built ten times.

The last step is the same either way. Sum the components, compare to the in-game cost, and if the numbers disagree the tooltip has something in it you have not accounted for.

It is the only slot that is optional, always available and cheap, so a leftover 5 lands there by default. That is not wrong — but it should never be described as a reason the build works.

Traps worth naming

  • Stacked −mSPD. A high-capacity magazine, a +REC4 grip and a thermal optic all charge the same stat, three slots deep, against a single counter in a fourth
  • Buying recoil on a spread-limited weapon. If spread growth is the ceiling, more +REC does not raise it
  • Treating a counterable drawback as free. −mACC can be cancelled, but only two +mACC sources exist — spending one to get back to zero caps your ceiling a tier lower than a clean grip would have
  • A projected beam on slow play. Any laser above the floor tier throws a beam roughly 5m ahead, which rounds corners before you do. Irrelevant if you push; a real tell if you hold angles
  • Assuming a magazine cost. Magazines swing 5 to 55 per weapon and are where published data is most often wrong. A single bad assumption can be 35 points

Tiered systems

Almost nothing in this game scales continuously. Stats move in discrete rungs, each buff or debuff shifts you exactly one rung, and the step size shrinks toward the strong end so diminishing returns are built into the ladder itself.

Every stat that has been examined is tiered. Treat an unfamiliar one the same way until something contradicts it.

ADS accuracy while moving  tier shifts

Not a value ladder but a tier-shift system, and the shifts sum. Every attachment granting +mACC moves you one tier better; every attachment carrying −mACC moves you one tier worse.

ShiftSources
−1 tier betterLight · Ext. Light · Short Light barrels  ·  50 MW Violet · 50 MW Blue · 120 MW Blue
+1 tier worseAny grip carrying −mACC — 12 of the 21
Three net tiers removes the penalty entirely

At +mACC ×3, firing while moving is identical to firing while standing still. Not reduced — eliminated. Moving ADS spread starts at 0.32° against 0.05° stationary, a 6.4× penalty on every shot taken while strafing, and three tiers collapse it to nothing.

Those two figures are the ADSStandMoveMin and ADSStandBaseMin values on sym.gg, listed per weapon in the base spread table on each weapon card.

Nothing else in the attachment system removes a drawback outright rather than trading it down.

The system is bounded at −3 and +1

The tier range runs from three better than base to one worse, and both ends are structural rather than arbitrary.

TierMeaning
−3Moving spread equals stationary spread
−2The practical ceiling — see routing below
−1One source, or one penalty cancelled
0Base moving spread, 6.4× stationary
+1A penalized grip. The worst case possible

The penalty cannot be stacked

−mACC exists in one slot only — the underbarrel, on 12 of its 21 grips. No muzzle, barrel, ammo, optic, accessory, ergonomics or magazine option carries it. Since a weapon has one underbarrel, +1 is the floor of the system and there is no way to be deeper. One +mACC source always fully cancels whatever grip you chose.

Routing, and where it stops

SlotSourceTiers
BarrelLight · Ext. Light · Short Light1
Accessory50 MW Violet · 50 MW Blue · 120 MW Blue1

Two is the reachable maximum. One barrel and one laser, and a second laser cannot be mounted. Every other slot has been checked and none of them grant +mACC.

So −3 is modeled but has no known attachment route. Either a source exists outside the nine slots — stance, class trait, or something weapon-specific — or the bottom rung is only reachable in circumstances the attachment system does not control. Until that is identified, build for −2 and treat −3 as unproven.

A −mACC grip drops you to −1 net even with both sources equipped, which is the real argument for the clean grip ceiling on a mobile build.

Seeing this for yourself

Dr. Smiley Henry's ADS Spread Simulator plots the spread circle shot by shot at every tier, for any weapon, standing or moving. It is the fastest way to see what a tier is actually worth on the gun you are building.

ADS movement speed  tier shifts

The same tier-shift system as moving accuracy, on its own axis. Every +mSPD source moves you one tier better, every −mSPD one tier worse, and the shifts sum.

ShiftSources
−1 tier betterLightweight ammo  ·  low-capacity magazines
+1 tier worse+REC4 and +REC5 grips  ·  high-capacity magazines  ·  multi-stage and thermal optics

This is the one stat three separate slots can charge at once, and it has a single counter in a fourth. A high-control, high-capacity, thermal build is three tiers down against one available tier back.

Design rules

1 · Attachment costs are pure arithmetic

Every cost is the sum of its components.

Drawbacks run the same prices in reverse. A drawback listed on an attachment discounts it by exactly what the matching benefit costs to buy — a grip that gives up moving accuracy is 10† cheaper, which is what moving accuracy costs to add anywhere else. Nothing is hidden in the pricing, and no attachment is a bargain.

† −SWAY, −VEL and −DRAW discount by 5.

So the game is not finding underpriced parts. It is deciding which drawbacks you can afford to carry, across the slots you have, for the way you actually play.

Worked examples
  • Alloy Vertical (20) = +REC2 (20)
  • Folding Vertical (10) = +REC2 (20) − accept −mACC (10)
  • Low-Profile Stubby (45) = +REC5 (55) − −mACC (10) − −mSPD (10) + +ADS (10)
  • Slim Angled (25) = +REC1 (10) + +DRAW (5) + +ADS (10)
  • QD Grip Pod (30) = +REC2 (20) + MOUNT (10)
  • Standard Suppressor (20) = Flash Hider (10) + HIDE-MAP (10), carrying −HIP
  • Lightened Suppressor (30) = Standard (20) + +RBLD (10)
  • Long Suppressor (25) = Lightened (30) − accept −SWAY (5)
  • Heavy Ext. barrel (10) = +SUSTAIN (10) + +VEL (5) − accept −DRAW (5)
  • Short barrel (15) = +HIP1 (10) + +ADS (10) − accept −VEL (5)
  • Flechette (30) = Frangible (20) + +PEN (10)
There are no value picks and no traps

Every point buys the same performance, so choose on effects rather than efficiency. Anything that does not fit the formula has a hidden effect you have not spotted.

Most attachments cost the same on every weapon that offers them. Muzzles, grips, optics, optic accessories, lasers, lights and ergonomics are fixed, and so are the barrel archetypes. Variance is confined to two places:

2 · You cannot buy clean recoil above +REC2 — but you can buy it back

Every grip above "slightly reduces recoil" carries −mACC, and +REC4 / +REC5 add −mSPD on top. Within the underbarrel slot, high control and mobile accuracy are mutually exclusive by design.

Both penalties have a counter, and both cost 10 points — in other slots.

PenaltyCounterSlotPts
−mACC50 MW Violet +mACC†Accessory10
−mSPDLightweight ammo +mSPDAmmo10

† A Blue laser also carries +mACC, but projects a beam.

Neither competes with the grip for space, and both cancel one for one. A single Violet laser fully absorbs one penalized grip; adding a Light barrel puts you a tier ahead of base. Lightweight ammo does the same job for −mSPD.

For a mobile build the grip choice costs you a tier of moving accuracy, not just points. Only two +mACC sources exist, so a clean grip caps you at −2 and a penalized one caps you at −1. Whether that tier is worth more than the recoil rung it buys depends entirely on how much of your shooting happens in motion.

Both drawbacks are recoverable, so the ceiling is a spending decision rather than a hard limit. Two routes land on the same total and deliver different things:

RouteSpendDelivers
Canted Stubby + Violet + Standard ammo45+REC3 +ADS, −mACC cancelled
6H64 Vertical + Violet + Lightweight ammo45+REC4, both −mACC and −mSPD cancelled

Identical cost, because Lightweight ammo is only 5 over the Standard you were paying for anyway. The 6H64 route buys an extra recoil rung and mSPD neutrality; the Canted Stubby route buys +ADS instead. Neither is strictly better — but the idea that high-rung grips are unaffordable for a mobile player does not survive the arithmetic.

Lightweight ammo also satisfies the ammo floor, so its true marginal cost over Standard is only 5.

3 · Moving ADS spread is already 6.4× worse than stationary

Base values are 0.32° moving vs 0.05° stationary. −mACC stacks on an already-bad number, making it more expensive than the tooltip implies. Costly to players who shoot while repositioning, nearly free to players who plant first — and removable outright at three net tiers of +mACC.

4 · Mounting is the only purchasable spread reduction

Measured at roughly 50% recoil and 50% spread reduction while mounted, and it applies whether you are moving or not. No grip comes close — the +REC ladder barely touches precision.

+mACC also reduces spread, but only the moving penalty, and only down to the base stationary figure. Mounting is the only thing that improves on standing still.

The untracked cost is tempo. Mounting takes roughly a second to mount, settle and fire, and locks you in place. Mountable surfaces are plentiful, but the delay appears in no stat.

5 · Suppressors do not affect muzzle velocity

The datamined Suppressed velocity column is blank for every assault rifle. Suppressors cost hipfire, sway, or points — never velocity or damage.

6 · Four slots have no "None" — every build starts 20 points down

Ammo, barrel, optic and magazine all require a selection. There is no zero-cost option in any of them.

SlotFloorCheapest option

Because the floors are unavoidable, upgrading within them is cheaper than it looks — an ammo or barrel upgrade costs only its difference over the floor.

What this means for you

  • You cannot look an attachment up by name across weapons. A name is not a stable identifier

Linear Comp is an axis converter, not a downgrade

It reduces horizontal recoil by 20–25% and increases vertical by roughly one rung. Vertical is a learnable pattern; horizontal is a random cone you cannot compensate for.

Take it above ~35° recoil direction variation. Skip it below ~20° — there's no horizontal left to convert and you just eat the vertical penalty.

The stealth economy

  • 10 pts (Flash Hider) → HIDE-3D HIDE-FLASH
  • 20–30 pts (any suppressor) → the above plus HIDE-MAP
  • Barrel slot on integrally suppressed weapons → HIDE-MAP at 20 or HIDE-MAP+ at 30
  • Subsonic ammo + any suppressor → HIDE-MAP+

The spotting diamond is cheap. The minimap costs exactly 10 more.

Flash Comp (20) is the efficient pick when minimap concealment isn't required — it is Flash Hider plus +RBLD, with no drawback. Long Suppressor (25) is Lightened (30) with −SWAY accepted for a 5-point discount.

Two grips buy capability, not stats

Underslung Mount (10) enables HE, Thermobaric and smoke grenade launchers to be mounted under the weapon. No stat penalty — the only cost is forfeiting a grip. This is the largest utility swing available in the slot.

Compact Handstop (10) enables firing while sprinting, with no listed drawback — but it is SMG only. It removes the sprint-to-fire delay entirely, which no amount of +ADS can replicate, and it is the reason an SMG can contest a corner an assault rifle has to stop for.

Penetration is free to add. It costs the same as Standard, so any "Pen." composite costs exactly what its other half costs. Combining two paid types instead sums them — Sub. HP is Subsonic plus Hollow Point at full price.

Penetration penalties do not stack. Subsonic and Hollow Point each carry −PEN; Sub. HP carries one, not two. And because Penetration's +PEN cancels Subsonic's −PEN, Sub. Pen is the only way to get spotting reduction without a penetration cost.

When Synthetic is offered, it is priced 5–10 points higher than Hollow Point on that weapon.

The most expensive optic in the game is priced by the formula

Premium feature (25) + remove −mSPD (10) = 35. TH-RDS 1.00x is a thermal sight that does not slow you down, and that is the entire reason it costs more than a 10.00x scope.

Only two features exist at this tier: extra magnification and thermal. Magnification itself remains free — a 10-point 8.00x sits alongside a 10-point 1.00x — so you are never paying for zoom, only for glass behavior.

ZTOGGLE costs 20 and carries nothing. ZCYCLE costs 25 and adds −mSPD — adjusting for the drawback, 15 points dearer for the extra magnifications.

Scope glint applies only to sniper rifles. Magnified optics on every other class are signature-free, so there is no concealment argument against magnification outside sniping.

The range-coverage system

Three of these solve the same problem — a fixed optic only works at one distance — and which one you're offered depends on where your optic already sits:

Your opticYou getResult
1.5x – 2xMAGNIFIERDoubles up: reach you didn't have
3x and abovePIGGYBACKDrops to 1x: close range you didn't have
Sniper scopesZTOGGLEA second magnification on demand

All three cost 10 points. The game charges a flat fee for "cover a second range band," and simply hands you whichever direction your optic is missing. Canted sights (5–10) do the same job mechanically but work on any optic and require a physical tilt rather than a toggle.

The split follows magnification: 1.5x–2x optics are offered the Magnifier, 3x and above the Piggyback. Which specific optics appear on a given weapon still varies.

Accessories — Lasers, Lights and Rangefinders

Mounting position varies by weapon — the same laser sits on the right rail of one gun and the top rail of another. Position is not slot type.

A weapon can have more than one accessory mount, so running a light and a laser simultaneously is possible. How many mounts you get is weapon-specific. Accessories do not share space with the underbarrel.

Lights

All three illuminate. What you pay for is when they turn on — the automated variants exist so your light is never lit in the stance where it would give you away.

Taclight — HIP is the concealment-aware option for anyone who holds angles: lit only in close quarters where you are likely already spotted, dark whenever you are aimed in at range. The 5 points over the Flashlight buy the automation, not the light.

Blue is Green plus +mACC for 10 points at both rungs. Red trades hipfire for a shorter beam; Violet spends its 10 points on +mACC instead of hipfire.

What "visible" actually means

−SEEN means the laser projects a beam into world space, readable by enemies for roughly 5m in front of you. The practical cost is positional: a beam can round a corner before you do, or spill through a doorway you have not stepped into yet. For anyone playing slowly and holding angles, that is a real tell — it announces where you are about to be.

Every laser is noticeable on the weapon itself, including Red and Violet. Looked at directly from any range, the emitter is bright and obvious. −SEEN is not the difference between hidden and seen — it is the difference between betraying yourself around cover and only being visible once someone already has eyes on you.

So Red and Violet are the positionally quiet options rather than invisible ones. If nothing on the build depends on the enemy failing to notice your gun, the 20–30 point tier is not disqualified — it just costs you the corner.

Aftermarket Buffer (5) reduces screen shake and sight movement without changing any underlying value. Nothing about the bullets changes, but you can see your own impacts and correct mid-burst.

Magwell Flare (10) and DLC Bolt (30) are the same feature split by class — reload without leaving your sight picture.

Three entries change or tune the weapon's fire mode, and all three are weapon-specific. A3 Receiver (25) converts a semi-auto to full auto and appears on the M16A4 alone. Burst Training (15) adds burst, and Match Trigger (15) improves an existing semi-auto — both are scattered across a handful of weapons. You are rarely choosing between them; on most weapons none are offered at all.

The barrel slot is where three component prices appear that no other slot exposes: +VEL at 5, +SUSTAIN at 10, and the −5 discount for accepting −VEL or −DRAW.

Integrally suppressed weapons buy concealment in the barrel slot

Weapons with an integral suppressor have no muzzle slot. Their barrel archetypes are the suppressor instead.

Every muzzle suppressor cuts minimap spot range to ~21m; the enhanced integral barrel cuts it to 9m on its own — the same figure a suppressor reaches when paired with Subsonic ammo.

Magazine — the model

Magazines cannot be archetyped the way barrels can. Capacities are weapon-specific and costs scale per weapon family, so a tag set copied from one weapon means nothing on another.

Tags mirror the magazine's own description. A magazine that says it handles faster carries +DRAW; one that says nothing about handling carries no tag. They are not measured against the stock magazine — that would invert the reading on weapons whose stock happens to be the smallest option or a FAST variant.

The model is universal even though the values are not.

Capacity is the currency

Within any weapon's list, cost and effects track capacity in one direction:

CapacityCostEffects
Low end5 floor+DRAW +mSPD +ADS — deeper cuts buy more rungs
Middle5–10Few or no handling tags
High end15–40−mSPD, scaling with the increase

In every other slot you pay points. Here you pay points and capacity together, and capacity is not a tag — which is why a 20-round magazine and a 30-round magazine can both cost 5 while the smaller one is strictly better on every tracked stat.

The FAST variant

Every capacity is offered in a standard and a FAST version. The FAST version always:

  • grants +RELOAD — measured at 13% faster
  • costs one −DRAW rung against its standard counterpart
  • costs +5 points, unless the standard version is already at the 5-point floor

Reading a magazine list

  1. Tag straight from each description — if it does not mention a stat, there is no tag
  2. The low-capacity options sit at the 5-point floor and buy handling rungs
  3. The high-capacity options cost −mSPD and scale in price
  4. Each FAST is its standard counterpart, one −DRAW rung slower, plus +RELOAD

Cross-slot interactions

The pricing formula prices each attachment in isolation. Builds are decided by what happens when they stack.

+ADS stacks across barrel and underbarrel

Both slots grant it independently, so a granting barrel plus an +ADS grip moves you two rungs, not one.

Grants +ADSBaseline
Basic · Short · Light · Cryo · Ext. Light · Short LightExtended · Heavy · Heavy Ext. · Suppressed

Stacking moving accuracy

Two slots grant +mACC, one tier each:

SlotSourceTiers
BarrelLight · Ext. Light · Short Light1
Accessory50 MW Violet · 50 MW Blue · 120 MW Blue1 each

Barrel plus one laser reaches −2, and that is the ceiling — a second laser cannot be mounted and no other slot grants +mACC. The −3 rung the simulator models has no known attachment route. Violet is the only source without a projected beam, so a stealth build gives up nothing positional to reach −2.

−mACC is underbarrel-only, so it can never exceed one tier — but that one tier is the difference between needing three +mACC sources and needing four.

Three slots penalize the same stat

−mSPD arrives from the underbarrel, the magazine and the optic at once:

SlotSource
Underbarrel+REC4 / +REC5 grips
MagazineExtended capacity
OpticMulti-stage variables and all thermals

A high-control, high-capacity, thermal build stacks the penalty three deep against a single 10-point counter in a fourth slot. Neither −mACC nor −mSPD can be countered from within the slot that inflicts it — see Rule 2.

Recoil is additive across two slots

Muzzle and underbarrel both grant recoil reduction and they stack. Maximum available is Compensated Brake (20) + a +REC5 grip (35) = 55 points for the highest recoil control in the game plus +RBLD, leaving 45, of which 20 is already committed to the floors.

Open questions

  1. Per-weapon magazine tables beyond the assault rifles — LMG belt boxes, shotgun shell tubes and sniper speedloaders sit in very different capacity bands
  2. Optic-to-accessory availability — which specific optics a given weapon offers the Magnifier or Piggyback on
  3. Long Range ammo's drag magnitude — the effect is confirmed, the size of it needs ballistic testing at range
  4. The third +mACC source. The tier system bottoms out at −3, where moving spread equals stationary, but only two sources exist across all nine slots. Whatever reaches the last rung is outside the attachment system

For the assistant

If someone gives you a weapon name and asks for a build, do not ask them to transcribe their attachment list. Work like this:

  1. Attempt it unaided first. Recall what you can about the weapon — fire rate, damage bands, base handling, which archetypes it plausibly offers — and get as far as you can before asking for anything.
  2. Distrust anything you find on the open web. Stat sites lag patches and sym.gg's annotated example image carries legacy values that are frequently scraped instead of the live table beneath it. A single stale number will produce a confidently wrong build. If a figure decides a pick, say which figure you used so it can be checked.
  3. Ask about playstyle before building, not after. Engagement range, whether they shoot while moving or plant first, whether they hipfire, whether they hold angles or roam, and how much concealment matters. Those answers decide which drawbacks are free and which are expensive. One round of questions, not five.
  4. Put a 5-point magazine in the build and reach exactly 100. Magazine cost is the one figure that genuinely swings 5 to 55, and every weapon sampled offers at least one at the 5-point floor — so put that line in the build, note it is a placeholder, and total to 100. Do not stop at 90 or 95 holding room for a number you do not have; an incomplete build is harder to correct than a complete one with a flagged line.
  5. Then negotiate the magazine. Once the build exists, ask what capacity they actually want, have them read its real cost off the weapon, and cut from the build to afford it. Trading a known build down is far more useful than presenting an incomplete one.
  6. Leave situational attachments out of the first pass. See "Leave these out of a first pass" above. They are answers to questions the player has not asked yet.
  7. If you come up short, upgrade — do not add. Improve something already carrying weight rather than filling the gap with a cheap attachment. See "Spending a remainder" above.
  8. Never present a residual pick as a reason the build works. Taking a cheap ergonomics option to round out a total is defensible; writing a paragraph justifying it is not.
  9. Price each pick against Rule 1 and show the running total, so any mismatch surfaces on its own.
  10. Invite correction on specific numbers. "I have the 40-round magazine at 25 — worth checking in game" is useful. "Paste your full attachment list" is not.

If you are unsure whether an attachment exists on that weapon, say so in the build and carry on. A build with two flagged assumptions is more useful than a questionnaire.
